Conceptual Anchor

The Expert's Decision Rules

Imagine a specialist who makes decisions using explicit rules: IF condition A and condition B are true, THEN conclude C. A traditional rule-based expert system stores this specialist knowledge and applies the rules systematically to known facts.

1 Simple Computer Support Rule

Known facts: - no_power_light - charger_connected Rule: IF no_power_light AND charger_connected THEN possible_power_supply_problem Conclusion: possible_power_supply_problem

Forward vs Backward Chaining

Expert systems may reason from available data toward conclusions, or start from a goal and work backward to find supporting facts.

Forward Chaining

Starts from known facts and repeatedly applies rules to derive new facts.

DATA → CONCLUSION
Forward Chaining Backward Chaining
Data-driven Goal-driven
Starts from known facts Starts from a hypothesis or query
“What can I conclude?” “Can this goal be proved?”
Rules derive new facts Rules identify facts needed to prove the goal

Advantages & Limitations

Advantages Limitations
Can preserve specialist knowledge. Knowledge acquisition and maintenance can be expensive.
Can provide fast and consistent rule-based decisions. Usually limited to a narrow problem domain.
Can store large amounts of facts and rules. Only as reliable as the knowledge entered into the system — GIGO.
Can operate whenever the computer system is available. May handle unusual situations poorly when no appropriate rule exists.
Can provide traceable reasoning when an explanation facility is present. Users may incorrectly assume the system is always correct.

Traditional Expert Systems ≠ Machine Learning

Traditional rule-based expert systems use an explicitly created knowledge base and inference rules. Machine-learning systems learn statistical patterns from data. Rule-based systems do not automatically learn new patterns from training data; their knowledge must be updated explicitly.

How Prolog Finds Answers

Two useful ideas explain Prolog behaviour: unification matches variables with values that satisfy a fact or rule, while backtracking lets Prolog return to an earlier choice and search for another solution.

Queryparent(charles, X).
UnificationMatch facts with the query pattern.
BacktrackingSearch again after the first answer.

X = harry

3 Computer Troubleshooting Knowledge Base

% FACTS no_power_light. charger_connected. fan_loud. computer_hot. wifi_connected. internet_not_working. % RULES problem(power_supply) :- no_power_light, charger_connected. problem(overheating) :- fan_loud, computer_hot. problem(network) :- wifi_connected, internet_not_working. % QUERY EXAMPLES % ?- problem(power_supply). % ?- problem(X).

Minimum project requirements

At least 5 facts · at least 3 rules · at least 3 queries · one query containing a variable · a short explanation of how one conclusion was reached.

Common Pitfalls

1. Atom vs Variable

john is an atom. John is a variable because it begins with uppercase.

2. Missing Full Stop

Prolog facts and rules normally end with .

3. Rule Direction

Read father(X,Y) :- male(X), parent(X,Y). as “father(X,Y) is true IF male(X) AND parent(X,Y) are true.”

4. Forward ≠ Backward Chaining

Forward chaining begins with facts; backward chaining begins with a goal or query.

5. Expert Systems Are Domain-Specific

A rule-based system can only reason with the knowledge represented in its knowledge base.

Exam Practice

Q1: State the purpose of the knowledge base in an expert system. [1]

Reveal marking point
Stores domain knowledge, such as facts and rules, that the inference engine can use.

Q2: Explain the role of the inference engine. [2]

Reveal marking points
It selects/applies relevant rules to known facts and uses logical reasoning to derive new facts or conclusions.

Q3: Explain one difference between forward and backward chaining. [2]

Reveal marking points
Forward chaining starts from known facts and derives conclusions; backward chaining starts from a goal/hypothesis and searches for facts/rules that prove it.

Q4: Explain two limitations of an expert system. [4]

Reveal marking points
Examples: expensive knowledge acquisition/maintenance; narrow domain; unusual cases may not match any rule; results depend on the quality of stored knowledge; users may place too much trust in the system.

Self-Check Quiz

Q1: What does the Knowledge Base store?

Domain knowledge such as facts and rules.

Q2: Which component applies rules to facts?

The Inference Engine.

Q3: Which chaining method starts with a goal?

Backward chaining.

Q4: What is the difference between john and John?

john is an atom; John is a variable.

Q5: What does :- mean?

It means “is true IF”.

Q6: What does a comma mean inside a Prolog rule?

Logical AND.

Q7: Why is the Explanation Facility useful?

It shows how a conclusion was reached or why the system asked a question.
