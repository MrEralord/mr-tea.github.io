Privacy Policies

A privacy policy explains what personal information an organisation collects, why it collects it, how the information is used and shared, how long it is kept, and what control users have over their data.

Learning Objectives

  • 12.1.3.3 Explain privacy policies
  • 12.1.3.4 Analyze problems arising due to disseminating and using information

Vocabulary Before Watching

Personal data

Information relating to an identifiable person.

Processing

Collecting, storing, using, changing, sharing or deleting data.

Consent

A clear agreement to a specified use where consent is appropriate.

Transparency

Being clear about what happens to people's information.

Retention

How long information is kept before it is deleted or anonymised.

Disclosure / sharing

Making information available to another person or organisation.

Personal Information

Personal information is information that relates to an identifiable person. Examples can include a name, email address, ID number, location history, device identifiers, examination results and biometric data.

The Data Journey

Personal information does not simply “sit in a database”. It moves through a lifecycle: it is collected, stored, used, sometimes shared, retained for a period of time, and eventually deleted or anonymised.

1 Data Journey

Collect Name, email, location, results...
Store Database, cloud service or other storage
Use Provide a service or perform a stated purpose
The data lifecycle continues
Share Only where appropriate and explained
Retain Keep for a justified period of time
Delete / anonymise Remove or de-identify when no longer needed

A good privacy policy should make important parts of this journey clear to the user.

What Is a Privacy Policy?

A privacy policy (or privacy notice) explains how an organisation handles personal information. It should be clear, accessible and specific enough for users to understand what will happen to their data.

Policy section Question it answers
Data collected What information do we collect?
Purpose Why do we need this information?
Use / processing How will the information be used?
Sharing Who may receive the information?
Security How is the information protected?
Retention How long will the information be kept?
User choices / rights What control does the user have?
Contact Who can the user contact about privacy?

2 Privacy Policy Anatomy

WHAT? Personal data collected
WHY? Purpose for collecting and using it
HOW? Processing and protection
A complete policy also explains
WHO? Sharing and recipients
HOW LONG? Retention period
YOUR CONTROL Choices, rights and contact

Good Privacy Principles

Collect only what is needed

Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.

Use it for a clear purpose

Personal information should be used for an explained and appropriate purpose.

Keep it accurate

Incorrect information can lead to unfair or harmful decisions.

Protect it

Use appropriate security measures against unauthorised access, loss or disclosure.

Do not keep it forever

Retention should be linked to a justified need.

Be transparent

Users should be able to understand how their information is handled.

Laws vary by jurisdiction

Privacy and data-protection laws differ between countries and regions. Examples include the EU GDPR and national data-protection legislation. This lesson focuses on the principles of a clear privacy policy rather than memorising one country's legal wording.

Example of a concise policy statement

We collect student names, classes and quiz results to provide learning activities and progress reports. Access is limited to authorised users. We do not sell student learning data to advertisers. Data is retained only for as long as needed for the educational service and applicable requirements.

Disseminating & Using Information

Dissemination of information means distributing information or making it available to other people or organisations. Problems can arise when information is shared too widely, used for an unexpected purpose, is inaccurate, or remains available after it should have been removed.

3 Information Risk Chain

Information Location, marks, photos, financial data...
Problem Over-sharing, misuse, inaccuracy or inappropriate disclosure
Consequence Privacy loss, unfair decisions, harm, profiling or loss of trust

Strong analysis follows the pattern: what information → what problem → what consequence.

Scenario Problem Possible consequence
Fitness app publishes a user's running route publicly Private location information is disseminated too widely Tracking, unwanted contact, safety risk, loss of privacy
Shopping app shares purchase history for an unexpected purpose Data is reused/shared beyond what the user reasonably expects Profiling, unwanted targeting, loss of trust
Incorrect personal record is used to make a decision Inaccurate information is treated as reliable Unfair decision, denied service, reputational or financial harm
Student results spreadsheet is posted publicly Confidential information is disclosed to unauthorised people Loss of privacy, embarrassment, reputational harm

Privacy vs Security

Security asks: “How do we protect information from threats?”
Privacy asks: “What information should be collected, used, shared and retained, and under what conditions?”

Common Pitfalls

Privacy ≠ security

Security protects information against threats. Privacy concerns the collection, use, sharing and retention of personal information.

Publicly visible ≠ unlimited use

Information being visible does not automatically make every later use, combination or redistribution appropriate.

Consent ≠ unlimited permission

Agreement to one clear purpose does not automatically justify every unrelated future use of the information.

Delete ≠ disappear everywhere

Once information has been disseminated, copies, screenshots or cached versions may remain even if the original post is removed.

Additional Information

Intellectual Property, Copyright, DRM & Software Licences

These ideas are related to responsible use of digital information, but they are not the core focus of the two privacy learning objectives on this page.

Term Concise meaning
Intellectual Property (IP) Creations of the mind that may receive legal protection.
Copyright Protects original creative works, including many digital works and software.
DRM Technology used to control access to or copying of digital content.
Commercial / proprietary Software distributed under restrictions set by its owner or vendor.
Shareware Software provided for evaluation or limited use under specified conditions.
Freeware Software available at no monetary cost, but still subject to licence terms.
Open source Software whose source code is available under a licence permitting specified forms of use, study, modification and redistribution.

Keep the distinction clear

Privacy is about handling information about people. Intellectual property is about rights relating to creative works, inventions, brands or other protected creations.

Exam Practice

Q1: Explain two items that should be included in a privacy policy. [4]

Reveal marking points
Any two suitable items, each with explanation. Examples: Data collected — identify what personal information is collected. [1+1] Purpose/use — explain why and how the information is processed. [1+1] Sharing — explain who may receive the information. [1+1] Retention — explain how long the information is kept. [1+1]

Q2: A fitness application shares detailed location histories with advertisers. Analyze two problems that may arise. [4]

Reveal marking points
One mark for each valid privacy problem and one mark for each linked consequence. Example: detailed location is disclosed to another organisation [1], which could enable tracking or create a personal-safety risk [1]. Example: data is used for an unexpected purpose [1], which may cause unwanted profiling/targeting or loss of trust [1].

Q3: Explain why inaccurate personal information can cause problems when it is used to make decisions. [2]

Reveal marking points
The decision is based on incorrect information. [1] This can lead to an unfair outcome, such as denial of a service or reputational/financial harm. [1]

Q4: Explain one difference between data privacy and data security. [2]

Reveal marking points
Privacy concerns whether and how personal information is collected, used, shared and retained. [1] Security concerns protecting information/systems against threats or unauthorised access. [1]
